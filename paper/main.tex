\begin{abstract}
Through-barrier speech direction-of-arrival estimation can fail with microphone arrays because the barrier acts as a distributed secondary radiator and same-room interference dominates microphone pressure. We fuse two microphones with a laser Doppler vibrometer that measures structure-borne vibration at a single barrier spot, providing a cleaner reference in our testbed. We introduce a zero-shot physics-informed geometric search that evaluates cross-modal GCC-PHAT between the vibrometer and each microphone at travel-time--predicted delays and enforces joint consistency across the two pairs. In a two-room setup with a cardboard barrier and a strong receiving-room jammer, the proposed method achieves $1.94^\circ$ MAE on a chirp reference and $2.23^\circ$ MAE on speech, while microphone-only baselines frequently collapse to spurious peaks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Direction-of-Arrival (DoA) estimation provides spatial cues that can steer multi-speaker speech pipelines, e.g., meeting diarization, speaker-attributed automatic speech recognition, and continuous speaker separation \cite{wang2021realtime_diar, mariotte2024asobo, taherian2024ssnd, park2024cssloc, shi2024spatial_ltmask}. Classical methods such as GCC-PHAT and SRP-PHAT \cite{knapp1976gcc, dibiase2001robust} and learning-based TDoA/DoA estimators \cite{sun2024msdet, berg2022shiftgcc, salvati2021pgccphat, shmuel2023deep, zhang2024robust} perform well in free-field or moderately reverberant environments using microphone arrays. However, homogeneous microphone-only sensing can break down in strongly attenuating through-barrier settings \cite{remillieux2009ldv, wang2024physics}.

The failure mode in through-barrier settings is practical and repeatable: attenuation and distributed re-radiation reduce usable spatial coherence and corrupt TDoA cues. If a jammer operates in the receiving room, mic-only features can be dominated by the unobstructed jammer \cite{adavanne2019seld, kulkarni2024doa}. In this paper we focus on a single dominant speech source behind a barrier under strong receiving-room interference. To address this, we explore an actively augmented sensing modality: the Laser Doppler Vibrometer (LDV). An LDV remotely measures surface velocity by detecting the Doppler shift of a reflected laser beam \cite{rothberg2017ldv}, capturing structure-borne vibration on the barrier \cite{reed2006ldvspeech, bocko2023estimating}. Recent optical-microphone studies explore LDV-based speech acquisition as a complementary channel under strong background noise and long distances \cite{lin2022mask_ldv, lin2023tbme_optical}. More broadly, heterogeneous vibration--air sensing has been explored for noise-robust speech enhancement and recognition \cite{wang2022icassp_bcac, wang2022taslp_bcac, miyagi2025ldv_asr, chen2025embc_ldv_ssl}. In practice, this vibration reference can be less sensitive to in-room airborne interference than microphone pressure, providing a useful anchor under low signal-to-jammer ratio (SJR) conditions.

In this paper, we propose a heterogeneous LDV-Microphone fusion framework for robust through-barrier DoA estimation. Rather than learning a barrier-specific mapping from raw, barrier-colored spectrograms, our primary contribution is a zero-shot Physics-Informed Geometric Search (PI-GS) algorithm. PI-GS computes cross-modal GCC-PHAT features between the LDV anchor and physical microphones and enforces joint geometric consistency, reducing sensitivity to magnitude coloration without training data. Through physical hardware evaluation against an adversarial near-field barrier, we show that PI-GS achieves $2.23^\circ$ MAE on blocked speech, while microphone-only baselines are strongly degraded by structural multipath. Furthermore, we demonstrate improved robustness under strong in-room jammers in the receiving room in our testbed.

\subsection{Microphone Vulnerability and Structural Error}
\label{sec:mic_vuln}

To locate a target using TDoA, standard algorithms rely on tracking time delays. In conventional free-space estimation, a point source $s(t)$ arrives at a microphone $m$ with a simple geometric delay $\tau_m$:
\begin{equation}
  x_{m}(t) = s(t - \tau_m) + n_m(t). \label{eq:free_space}
\end{equation}
When an obstacle is introduced, algorithms commonly mis-model it as a simple scalar transfer function $h_{\text{barrier}}(t)$ that merely colors the spectrum without destroying the spatial geometry:
\begin{equation}
  x_{m}(t) = \left[ s(t - \tau_m) * h_{\text{barrier}}(t) \right] + n_m(t). \label{eq:naive_wall}
\end{equation}
If Eq.~\ref{eq:naive_wall} held true, standard cross-correlation could still extract $\tau_m$. However, acoustic propagation through a barrier is often a two-stage process: the source wave excites the structure, and the excited surface then re-radiates sound into the room. Unlike a far-field point source, the near-field barrier behaves as a distributed secondary radiator. Let $v(\vec{r}_w, t)$ be the structural velocity at point $\vec{r}_w$ on the barrier surface $S$. The resulting near-field acoustic pressure received by a microphone at $\vec{r}_m$ can be expressed (up to a proportionality constant) via the Rayleigh surface integral:
%
\begin{equation}
  x_{m}(t) = a_m^{s} \iint_S v\left(\vec{r}_w, t - \frac{|\vec{r}_w - \vec{r}_m|}{c}\right) \frac{d\vec{r}_w}{|\vec{r}_w - \vec{r}_m|} + i_m(t), \label{eq:mics}
\end{equation}
%
where $a_m^s$ encapsulates the acoustic radiation scaling, and $i_m(t)$ represents local in-room interference (including the jammer $j(t)$) and sensor noise. This spatial integration can inject frequency-dependent phase distortions into the inter-channel TDoA, making mic-only localization unreliable even without a jammer, and worse under strong in-room interference.

\subsection{LDV Anchor Isolation}
\label{sec:ldv_isol}

Conversely, the LDV queries the structure-borne vibration at a singular, discrete surface point $\vec{r}_{\text{ldv}} \in S$:
%
\begin{equation}
  x_{\text{ldv}}(t)  = v(\vec{r}_{\text{ldv}}, t) + n_{\text{ldv}}(t)
  \label{eq:ldv}
\end{equation}
%
Contrasting this point measurement against the 2D surface integration of Eq.~\ref{eq:mics} highlights a key advantage of the LDV: it measures a localized barrier response rather than a surface-integrated acoustic field. Moreover, due to air--structure impedance mismatch and the barrier's mechanical dynamics, in-room airborne jammer waves often couple less efficiently into the measured surface vibration than into microphone pressure. As a result, the LDV can provide a cleaner target reference in low-SJR conditions in our setup, although we do not assume perfect isolation.

The dual combination of spatial phase blurring and spectral distortion poses a critical challenge. Feeding raw audio into generic deep learning models can encourage memorizing barrier-specific acoustic colorations and geometry \cite{ueno2023physics, olivieri2024physics}. To improve zero-shot robustness, our framework avoids black-box training and uses a purely analytical geometric solver (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}).

\textit{Scope and assumptions:} We target a single dominant source behind a barrier and use the LDV as a reference anchor that can have a higher effective SJR in our setup. PI-GS does not claim universal barrier inversion; instead, it is an engineering method that succeeds when the LDV--microphone pairs retain a usable LDV-coherent lag structure in the analysis band after time-frequency aggregation. Let $X_v(f)$ denote the STFT of $x_{\text{ldv}}(t)$ and $X_m(f)$ the STFT of microphone $m$. We denote the LDV channel by the subscript $V$. PI-GS computes $R_{VL}(\tau)$ and $R_{VR}(\tau)$ and scores each candidate $\mathbf{p}$ by evaluating these functions at predicted relative lags.

\subsection{Cross-Modal GCC Features}
\label{sec:gcc_features}

By discarding magnitude information, GCC-PHAT reduces sensitivity to magnitude coloration and emphasizes phase-consistent structure \cite{yamada2023speech}. We compute the Generalized Cross-Correlation with Phase Transform (GCC-PHAT) \cite{knapp1976gcc} between the LDV STFT $X_v(f)$ and each microphone STFT $X_m(f)$:
%
\begin{equation}
  R_{Vm}(\tau) = \int \frac{X_v(f)\,X_m^*(f)}{\left|X_v(f)\,X_m^*(f)\right|}\,e^{j2\pi f\tau}\,df, \quad m \in \{L, R\}
  \label{eq:gcc_vm}
\end{equation}
%
To connect Eq.~\ref{eq:gcc_vm} to the heterogeneous sensing physics, we separate the microphone observation into an LDV-coherent component (originating from a neighborhood of $\vec{r}_{\text{ldv}}$) and a residual term that aggregates the remaining barrier surface and in-room interference:
%
\begin{equation}
  X_m(f) = U_{\text{ldv}}(f)\,B_m(f)\,e^{-j 2\pi f |\vec{r}_{\text{ldv}} - \vec{r}_m|/c} + X_{m,\text{rest}}(f),
  \label{eq:cross}
\end{equation}
%
where $U_{\text{ldv}}(f)$ is the (unknown) barrier response at $\vec{r}_{\text{ldv}}$ driven by the source, $B_m(f)$ lumps slowly varying radiation/geometry effects, and $X_{m,\text{rest}}(f)$ aggregates the remaining barrier re-radiation and in-room interference. Under Eq.~\ref{eq:cross}, PHAT-GCC does not \emph{guarantee} barrier removal; it is useful when the LDV-coherent component is non-negligible after time-frequency aggregation. Fig.~\ref{fig:spatial_score} illustrates this coherence-trap collapse in a representative blocked window: mic--mic GCC-PHAT peaks near $\tau \approx 0$, while the PI-GS score remains peaked near the true coordinate.

To overcome this corrupted landscape, our algorithm acts as a \textit{Physics-Informed Geometric Search} (PI-GS), leveraging geometric consistency between $\tau_{VL}(\mathbf{p})$ and $\tau_{VR}(\mathbf{p})$ as a constraint.

\subsection{Zero-Shot Joint Search with an LDV Anchor}
\label{sec:joint_search}

PI-GS uses the LDV as a shared anchor and combines two cross-modal features (LDV--MicL and LDV--MicR) with a joint geometric consistency constraint.

\textit{Intuition (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}):} Each candidate location $\mathbf{p}$ implies two relative delays, $\tau_{VL}(\mathbf{p})$ and $\tau_{VR}(\mathbf{p})$. Fixing a delay value traces an iso-delay contour in the search region $\Omega$; when $|R_{Vm}(\tau)|$ exhibits a peak at some $\tau$, it effectively ``lights up'' the corresponding contour into a ridge. The source estimate is where ridges from both pairs agree at the same $\mathbf{p}$, which motivates maximizing a joint score $S(\mathbf{p})$ over $\Omega$. In a near-field scenario, defining the delay purely by target angle ($\theta$) implicitly assumes a known fixed distance. PI-GS instead searches a 2D source coordinate $\mathbf{p} = (X, Y)$ within a physically feasible search region $\Omega$ (dashed box in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}), where $X$ is parallel to the barrier and $Y$ is the range (normal) direction. For any hypothesized $\mathbf{p}$, we compute a free-space travel-time template to the LDV point and to each microphone, $\tau_V(\mathbf{p})$ and $\tau_m(\mathbf{p})$, using Euclidean distances and sound speed $c$, and form the relative cross-modal delay template $\tau_{Vm}(\mathbf{p}) = \tau_m(\mathbf{p}) - \tau_V(\mathbf{p})$ for $m \in \{L, R\}$. We denote these as $\tau_{VL}(\mathbf{p})$ and $\tau_{VR}(\mathbf{p})$ for notational clarity. For each pair, the level set $\{\mathbf{p}:\tau_{Vm}(\mathbf{p})=\text{const}\}$ traces an iso-delay contour in $\Omega$ (faint curves in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). Although Eq.~\ref{eq:mics} implies the microphone observation is a distributed re-radiation field, this template provides an engineering constraint: PI-GS succeeds when the LDV-coherent component yields a dominant lag that varies consistently with $\mathbf{p}$ across both microphones.

\textit{Evaluation convention (testbed):} In our cardboard setup, the ground-truth source locations are specified by a lateral coordinate $X=x_s$ along the barrier, with a fixed range in our evaluation. We therefore visualize and evaluate 1D slices of the objective along $X$ (with $Y$ fixed). For reporting DoA in degrees, we use the standard mic-pair convention with spacing $d$ ($d=1.4$\,m in our setup): after obtaining $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$, we compute the implied mic-pair delay $\tau_{LR}(\hat{\mathbf{p}})=\tau_L(\hat{\mathbf{p}})-\tau_R(\hat{\mathbf{p}})$ and map it to an equivalent azimuth angle $\hat{\theta}=\arcsin(c\,\tau_{LR}(\hat{\mathbf{p}})/d)$. The PI-GS core operation establishes a unified joint scoring objective across the LDV anchor. Each candidate $\mathbf{p}$ predicts two relative delays and indexes into the two GCC-PHAT magnitudes, producing a joint score
$S(\mathbf{p}) = \left|R_{VL}\big(\tau_{VL}(\mathbf{p})\big)\right| + \left|R_{VR}\big(\tau_{VR}(\mathbf{p})\big)\right|$.
Geometrically, peaks in $|R_{Vm}(\tau)|$ appear as ridges along iso-delay contours in $\Omega$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). We estimate the source location by maximizing $S(\mathbf{p})$ over $\Omega$:
%
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{p}} = \arg\max_{\mathbf{p} \in \Omega} S(\mathbf{p})
  \label{eq:obj}
\end{equation}
%
Multipath can create spurious peaks in either correlation (e.g., $H_L'$), but they rarely agree for the same $\mathbf{p}$ under the shared LDV anchor. Under the condition that each $|R_{Vm}(\tau)|$ exhibits a salient peak near the true relative delay $\tau_{Vm}(\mathbf{p}^\star)$, the maximizer $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ tends to concentrate near the intersection of the dominant ridges.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

To validate the proposed heterogeneous system under severe attenuation, we construct a physical testbed mimicking a search-and-rescue or surveillance scenario.

\subsection{Acoustic Environment and Hardware}
A standard cardboard partition ($120\times 90$\,cm) separates the source and receiving rooms. In our testbed, the barrier acts as a broadband ``Coherence Trap'' (i.e., distributed re-radiation that corrupts mic-only delay cues) via near-field diffraction. A studio monitor plays LibriSpeech segments \cite{panayotov2015librispeech} (six speakers, ten utterances each). To simulate worst-case near-field occlusion, the barrier is placed $25\text{\,cm}$ from the target speaker across five azimuth angles (spanning $\pm20.8^\circ$). To support segment-level evaluation, we also record a separate chirp reference at the same nominal positions, with the chirp played continuously three times. Table~\ref{tab:validation} reports errors on the first chirp reference slice (0--2\,s) and on a speech slice (3--8\,s) from separate recordings. In the receiving room, two measurement microphones are placed $1.75\text{\,m}$ from the barrier with a wide $1.4\text{\,m}$ spacing, exacerbating spatial mismatch. The LDV is an Optomet Vector-series unit targeting the barrier surface opposite the speaker's axis. To evaluate interference robustness, a "jammer" speaker broadcasts continuous babble noise inside the receiving room, $1.0\text{\,m}$ from the microphones. Due to the barrier's significant transmission loss, the \emph{effective} Signal-to-Jammer Ratio (SJR) at the microphones is profoundly negative even at moderate jammer volumes.

\subsection{Implementation Details}
Signals are acquired at a hardware sample rate of 48\,kHz. To extract spatial features, we compute GCC-PHAT from STFTs with $n_{\text{fft}}=1024$ and aggregate the resulting correlation features over 0.5-second windows. PI-GS then sweeps the physical coordinate manifold $\Omega$ using the joint objective in Eq.~\ref{eq:obj} without requiring neural network pre-training.

Table~\ref{tab:validation} shows that microphone-only localization can be severely misleading in blocked settings on both the chirp reference and speech slices: Mic--Mic GCC-PHAT is often captured by spurious structural peaks, yielding $31.49^\circ$ MAE on the chirp reference and $35.79^\circ$ MAE on speech, with errors up to $74.47^\circ$ (chirp) and $76.56^\circ$ (speech). In this setup, PI-GS achieves $1.94^\circ$ MAE on the chirp reference and $2.23^\circ$ MAE on speech across the five blocked target locations, with a worst-case error of $4.18^\circ$ on blocked speech. This control suggests that the blocked-condition failures are driven by barrier-induced distributed re-radiation and the resulting multipath-dominated correlation landscape. In contrast, PI-GS reduces sensitivity to these effects by leveraging an LDV anchor and a joint geometric consistency constraint, improving stability across incidence angles in our experiments.

Beyond tracking accuracy through passive attenuation, the primary motivation for integrating the LDV is robustness against severe near-field airborne interference. To quantify this, we sweep the Signal-to-Jammer Ratio (SJR) of an independent babble-noise interferer operating within the receiving room during the ``blocked'' speech trial (Fig.~\ref{fig:jammer_curve}). 
This stress test complements robustness considerations in recent multi-speaker speech pipelines that rely on spatial cues under interference \cite{wechsler2023multi, zhang2024tse}, and aligns with DoA estimation studies in non-ideal interference regimes \cite{feintuch2023neural}. As the interference intensity dominates the receiving room (SJR decreases toward $-20$\,dB), the microphone-only paradigm suffers geometric capture, being dragged away from the attenuated structure target and tending to lock onto the local jammer's orientation (MAE $\approx 31$--$34^\circ$ in this experimental geometry). Because the jammer out-powers the barrier's structure-borne component in the microphones, the Mic--Mic GCC-PHAT peak is predominantly determined by the interference field. In contrast, the PI-GS estimation remains anchored below $\approx 1.3^\circ$ across the entire evaluated SJR sweep. In our physical testbed, the airborne jammer directly dominates the microphone pressure, whereas the LDV experiences natural physical isolation from the in-room interference due to air--structure impedance mismatch. Therefore, the LDV anchor preserves a usable LDV-coherent component in $R_{VL}(\tau)$ and $R_{VR}(\tau)$ under time-frequency aggregation. Thus, by utilizing the LDV as our joint optimization anchor (Eq.~\ref{eq:obj}), PI-GS reduces susceptibility to in-room jammer peaks without requiring explicit signal separation heuristics.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a heterogeneous sensing framework that utilizes a Laser Doppler Vibrometer as a physical anchor for through-barrier DoA estimation. To combat spatial blurring induced by barrier re-radiation, we developed a Zero-Shot Physics-Informed Geometric Search (PI-GS) that processes cross-modal GCC-PHAT features, avoiding black-box training. PI-GS is an engineering solver and does not claim universal barrier inversion; it is effective when the LDV--microphone pairs retain a usable LDV-coherent lag structure in the analysis band. Experimental evaluations demonstrate that while conventional microphone arrays can fail under severe attenuation and structural multipath, PI-GS reduces sensitivity to these distortions, achieving $1.94^\circ$ MAE on a chirp reference and $2.23^\circ$ MAE on blocked speech, with strong robustness under in-room jammers in our testbed. Future work will formalize information-theoretic lower bounds governing structure-borne vs.\ air-borne spatial coherence across diverse barrier materials.
